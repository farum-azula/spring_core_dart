## Unreleased

- The first release. `@Signal()` on a field of a component (its readers are told of an assignment; a `List`, `Set`, `Map` or `Queue` also of a change in place), `@Computed()` on a getter or a method without parameters (a value kept until what it read changes), `@Effect()` on a method (run again when what it read changes). The fields stay plain fields.
- Asynchronous state: a `@Computed` getter that returns a `Future` or a `Stream` is loaded once and again when what it read before its first `await` changes; `books.state` (`AsyncValue`), `books.refresh()`, `books.reload()`; an answer that came after a newer request is dropped.
- A method of such a bean is one change, whatever it assigns. `untracked` and `batch` are exported.
- Beside other advice on the same bean (`@Transactional`, `@Cacheable`, an aspect) the advice that follows state has an order of its own (`signalsAdvisorOrder`, one step before the default): on a `@Computed` member the other advice runs when the value is computed, not for each read of the kept one. A rollback does not undo the fields.
- An error is the bean's own: a `@Computed` getter that throws fails its reader with what was thrown (not the wrapper of the signals package), and the failure is not kept (the next read computes again). An effect that throws after its first run is reported (`FlutterError.reportError`) instead of failing the assignment that ran it, and goes on following what it followed; one that throws as the bean is made stops the bean from being made. Other advice on such a bean (`@Transactional`, `@PreAuthorize`) skips the bean's own calls, as in any bean. A `late` field read before it is assigned tells its reader of the first assignment. A collection is followed in place by the field's declared type (`List`, `Set`, `Map`, `Queue`); a field declared as a collection class of its own keeps that class.
- Widgets: `SpringStatelessWidget` and `SpringState` are built again for what their `build` read, by field; `Watch` does the same for ordinary widgets; `AsyncView` shows an asynchronous value.
- The state ends with the bean (its scope is its lifetime); the bean's own `@PreDestroy` methods still read its fields.
- The State tab of the debug panel (`spring.flutter.signals.debug.enabled`): every bean and member by name, with values and how often each changed, loaded or ran; `@Signal(hidden: true)` keeps a value out of it and of the log (`spring.flutter.signals`).
