## 0.10.0

- Fixed: an `@Effect` whose first run threw (the bean was not made) stayed subscribed and ran again on the half-made bean; the bean's state now ends at once (its effects and watches stop) and the error goes on to the container.
- Fixed: `refresh()` / `reload()` could wait for ever: when asked for again while one ran (the first now ends with the newer load), when the bean closed meanwhile, when a stream ended without an event, when the computation gave the same future again or a plain value.
- Fixed: the debug tools kept one key per effect name, so two beans of one class (prototypes, scoped beans) shared an effect node, its edges, lamp and times; an effect is now keyed by its bean (`SignalBean.effectFollowers`, `SignalProbe.ran` takes the `EffectFollower`; `SignalsDebugRecorder.nodeOf` is gone).
- Fixed: `SignalsDebugRecorder` kept the members of closed beans, stopped effects and their counts for as long as it lived; `pruneEnded` now forgets them (also from the console's `signals` segment as it samples), and everything collected is forgotten when the last tool hides.
- Fixed: a field whose write was deferred past the end of its scope (`on-scope-end: defer`) and then told at once (an `immediate` queue) or left with nothing to tell (the same value) was freed only at `defer-timeout`, and then logged as discarded.
- Fixed: with `on-overflow: flush`, a queue at its capacity took every write made while it was telling its readers, past its capacity; such a write is now refused as with `drop-newest`.
- Fixed: the Signals tab could show the value of a `@Computed` member that its own first read made the caller's; it now shows the lock.
- Fixed: `SignalBeans` kept a reference for every prototype ever made until a debug tool read `live`; the references of states nobody holds are dropped every 64 new beans.
- Fixed: `signalWidgetNames` named every Spring widget and `Watch` (type, key, number, the widget a `Watch` is in) while every tool was closed. A widget is now named only while a tool collects (`SignalWidgetNames.start` / `stop`, from `SignalsDebugRecorder.use`, `SignalsDebugRecorder(names:)` in place of `onStart`); when one starts, the Spring widgets and `Watch`es on the screen are built once again. The bean is made only when a view of it is in the running app (the Signals tab, the `signals` pane, the Graph tab, the `graph` pane).
- Removed: `SignalsDebugSection.describe`, used only by tests; the tab renders `SignalsRow.member`.
- Removed: `SignalsDebugRecorder.window`, `topChanged`, `topRead` and the `spring.flutter.signals.debug.window` property, which nothing showed.
- The debug tools write values as the Flutter starter's do (`DebugValueText.shortOf`; `SignalsValues.short` calls it): a text quoted, with the secrets of its addresses masked. `Watch` and `AsyncView` are registered as builders of the application graph (`DebugGraphBuilders`, bean `signalsGraphBuilders`), not names a reader is called after, by the modules whose view names widgets. The signals layer takes its colour through `DebugStyle.graphLayer('signals', orElse: command)`.
- `@Signal`, `@Computed`, `@Effect`, `@OnWatched` and `@OnUnwatched` are marked `@AccessHook` (was `@ProxyTargetClass`, renamed in spring_core_dart).

- Fixed: a `@Signal` field or `@Computed` value first used after its bean was closed (a `@PreDestroy` method reading it) was made as a live member that nothing freed; it is now made closed, reading and writing the bean's own field or code.
- Fixed: a `@Computed` value read after its bean was destroyed (a route-scoped bean, read by a widget in its own teardown) threw `A Signal<AsyncValue<Object?>> signal was written after being disposed`. A closed `@Computed` gives its last value (a future or stream: the last one, with no new load), `.state` the last state, without a signal read or written; `refresh()` / `reload()` fail with `StateError` naming the closed bean.
- The signals layer of the graph is drawn in the debug theme's `graph-signals` (orange by default), tells its kinds in the legend (`DebugGraphKind.description`) and its flash (`a signal ticked or an effect ran`); the Signals tab's graph keeps its state while the app runs (`signalsGraphLayer.viewMemory`, `buildView(memory:)`), the console's `signals` pane its own.
- The signals layer groups by the navigator stack with the application graph's route grouping (`SignalsGraphLayer(routes:)`, the default of the app's graph): a bean's members in the lane of the route whose scope holds the bean (`App` for a singleton), a reader widget in the lane of its route; `Group by: Module` keeps a lane per bean and the `Widgets` lane.

- With the Flutter starter's widgets layer in the build (`widgetsGraphLayer`), each widget that read state is merged into that layer's node of it when both layers show (`DebugGraphNode.sameAs` = `widgetsGraphLayer.nodeIdOf(element)`, the shared `DebugGraphWidgets.idOf` id): one node, the edges from what it read to it, its reads told in its detail; a `Watch` joins the widget it is in. Without the widgets layer shown, the readers are nodes of their own as before (`SignalsGraphLayer(widgets:)`).
- A `Watch` outside a Spring widget is named after the nearest widget above it that is not one of the framework's builders (`Watch in _Page`), else the nearest of a public type (`Watch in Column`), `Watch` alone at the root; never `?` or `a widget`. `NamedSignalTracking` keeps its widget's element (`context`, for the graph's "On screen" scope) and whether it is a `Watch`'s (`watch`).
- The signals graph, its list and the console's `signals` pane show the readers of an app built before the tool opened: when a tool starts collecting, every widget on the screen that `signalWidgetNames` named is built once again (`SignalsDebugRecorder(names:)`, `SignalWidgetNames.refreshAll`, `SignalTracking.refresh`), so what it reads is heard even when nothing changes under the debug panel. Before, a widget was a reader only once it was built again while the tool showed, so a covered, idle page showed its signals alone. A `Watch` is named too, after the widget it is in (`Watch in PageBackdrop`, `SignalWidgetNames.current`, set only with the debug tools in the build); a widget whose last build read nothing is no reader in the graph.
- The Signals tab's columns are as wide as their headers in the debug font (`SignalsTable.columnWidths`): `changed` no longer wraps; a header never takes two lines.
- The debug console's `signals` pane (`SignalsPane`, `signalsPane`, `@ConsolePane('signals', order: 20)`): the console's application graph (`DebugGraphPane`) preset to the signals layer, compact, on the console's own background: flashes and pulses on ticks, the scope, `Live only` and the bean lanes, a tapped node in one line. Kept as a thin pane beside the Flutter starter's `graph` pane so that `console.panes: [signals]` and `shown-panes: [signals]` keep working. Its module (`springBootStarterFlutterSignalsDebugConsolePaneAutoConfiguration`) is in the build only with the console and when `spring.flutter.debug.console.panes` names `signals` or holds `*`; it collects only while it shows. `signalWidgetNames` comes with the pane's module too, and no longer depends on `spring.flutter.signals.debug.enabled` at run time.
- The debug panel's State tab is the **Signals** tab. Its members are in columns, `value | changed | told | changes | writes | ticks | reads/5s | time`: `changed` the time since a real change, `told` since the readers were told (moved once a second), short values (a type's name instead of `Instance of 'X'`, `Map(1)`, `List(3)`, `Set(0)`, a long text cut; a tap on a collection shows its first items) in the debug theme's `number` colour and bold, a failure red, a load amber, zero counts and labels muted; a hidden, secret-looking or caller-bound value is a lock with its reason (`SignalsLock`), never the value. A row that changed in the last two seconds is lit and fades (`SignalsRowHighlight`). The row data is `SignalsRow`, the formatting `SignalsValues`.
- The signals are a layer of the Flutter starter's application graph (`SignalsGraphLayer`, bean `signalsGraphLayer`, a `DebugGraphLayer`): `@Signal` fields, `@Computed` values and `@Effect` methods in a lane per bean, each kind its own shape in the layer's colour (the debug theme's `logger`), the widgets that read them in the `Widgets` lane (a Spring widget and a `Watch` apart), those of one type folded into one plate (`ReviewTile ×10`) until tapped open; a tick flashes the node and runs a pulse along its edges. It collects only while a view shows it (`start`: the recorder's probe, what reads what checked twice a second, `changes` told then). Its module (`springBootStarterFlutterSignalsDebugGraphAutoConfiguration`) brings the panel's Graph tab: in the build by `spring.flutter.debug.graph.layers` (`signals`, `*`, not set), inside `spring.flutter.debug.graph.enabled`, `spring.flutter.signals.debug.enabled` and `spring.flutter.debug.enabled`; with the console, `springBootStarterFlutterSignalsDebugGraphPaneAutoConfiguration` brings the `graph` pane when `console.panes` names `graph`. The Signals tab has sub-tabs **Fields** (the columns, queues and buffers) | **Graph** (the application graph with this layer alone, only when the build has the layer) | **List** (the dependencies as an indented list). The package's own graph (`SignalsGraphView`, `SignalsGraphModel`, `SignalsGraphLayout`, `SignalsGraphScene`, `SignalsNodeDetails`) is gone: the engine is the Flutter starter's.
- Widgets by name: `ReviewTile`, `ReviewTile #3` while several are on the screen, `ReviewTile [42]` by a key that is a number, an enum or a plain word (never a text that could be data), with their rebuilds over the read window (`MatchHeader · 4 rebuilds/5s`). The names come from `signalWidgetNames` (`SignalWidgetNames`, `NamedSignalTracking`), a bean of the tab's module: without the tab in the build `signalBuildTracker` keeps nothing of any widget. `SignalBuildTracker` implements `WidgetAwareBuildTracker`; `SignalTracking` is a `base class` with `label` and `disposed`.
- `SignalsDebugRecorder`: `now`, `changedAt`, `toldAt`, `litAt`, `rebuildsOf`, `lampAt`, `lastLit`, `onActivity`, `structure`, `pruneEnded` (the widgets that left the screen are forgotten).
- The tab no longer shows the value of an asynchronous `@Computed` whose name looks secret.
- The console's `signals` segment names what it counts, `signals 3 ticks · 1 flush · 2 effects`, a zero muted.
- The debug panel's Signals tab is listed under the menu's `Logs & state` group (`GroupedDebugPanelSection`).
- What is not shown is not built: the `signals` segment is a module of its own (`springBootStarterFlutterSignalsDebugConsoleAutoConfiguration`), in the build only with the console and when `spring.flutter.debug.console.segments` names `signals` or holds `*`. The State tab's module has the debug panel's switch (`spring.flutter.debug.enabled`) as its parent: a build without the panel has none of its code. The segment is named by `@ConsoleSegment('signals')` in its bean definition; it extends the console's base class and has no `name` getter. There is no console command of the starter: the most often changed signals are in the Signals tab.
- Debug tools, each on its own switch and collecting only while it shows: the Signals tab gains the tree of who reads what with lamps, the times of computations and effects, counts of writes, publications and reads over a window, the queues, the open buffers and each bean's scope; in the build with the debug console (`spring.flutter.debug.console.enabled: true`), its `signals` segment (ticks, flushes and effect runs per frame), in place of a separate overlay; the DEBUG activity log (`spring.flutter.signals.debug.log.enabled`, logger `spring.flutter.signals.activity`, names only). `SignalProbe` and `SignalProbes`: what the tools hear, one static check on each read and write without them.
- `signalEffects` takes `signalBeans` and `signalRuntime` on first use: made with it, they (and `signalsProperties`, `defaultValidator`) were made before the other post-processors and skipped by them.
- **Security fix:** every later run of an `@Effect` and every `@OnWatched` / `@OnUnwatched` call runs as the user its bean was made for (a singleton: the program-wide user of the moment), never as the user whose change, read or commit ran it; a value that user may not read stays refused, whoever computed it. A reader refused before a guarded `@Computed` value was ever computed runs again when the user changes or when the value is first computed, and follows it from then on.
- **Breaking:** a bean on signals is made as a generated subclass (`<lib>.spring.signals.dart`) whose `@Signal` and `@Computed` members go straight to their signals, with no advice chain. A bean with advice gets the ordinary wrapper proxy over it; a bean with only signals has no proxy. `this.x` skips advice and is followed; a call through a `@Lazy` self reference is advised.
- **Breaking:** advice on a bean with signals is a build error unless `spring.flutter.signals.allow-advice: true`.
- **Breaking:** writes are held by a `SignalQueue` until their readers are told: `spring.flutter.signals.queue.flush` is `microtask` by default (also `immediate`, `frame`, `interval`), with `capacity`, `on-overflow` and named `spring.flutter.signals.queues.<name>.*`. The schedule is a function type, `SignalFlushSchedule`, called on every write (so a debounce can be written; the built-ins ignore a write while a flush is due, and the queue calls the cancel of the last call when it closes): a bean of it with `@Qualifier('<queue>')` is the schedule of that queue (both a bean and `flush:` for one queue fail the start). A method is no longer a batch by itself: with `microtask` one synchronous run is one change, with `immediate` use `batch`.
- **Breaking:** on a `@Computed` member the other advice runs for each call through the proxy, as on any bean; the value is kept inside it.
- `SignalBuffer`: holds the `@Signal` writes of async work in its zone; its own code reads them, everybody else sees the old values, and at its end they go to their queues as one change (dropped on an error, or told with `spring.flutter.signals.buffer.on-error: publish`). `@SignalBuffer()` on an async method of a bean on signals (`spring.flutter.signals.buffer.async-methods: true` for all of them, `@SignalBuffer(false)` to leave one out), `SignalBuffer.run` for other code (its `publishOnError` is its own; `SignalRuntime.buffered` uses the application's); a nested one joins the outer. Every setting belongs to its application: two in one process do not affect each other. A transaction opens one through the `TransactionLifecycleRegistry` bean (published on commit, dropped on rollback, a savepoint as a buffer inside it, followed collections copied); `spring.flutter.signals.transaction.mode: direct` writes at once. The signals starter and the data starter still do not depend on each other.
- `@Signal` options: `hidden`, `equals`, `queued`, `queue`, `eager`, `followCollection`, `onScopeEnd` (`discard` with a warning naming bean and fields, `flush`, `defer` with `defer-timeout`). A value equal by `==` but another object is kept, without a tick.
- `@OnWatched` and `@OnUnwatched` on methods, for a list of signals: one group or `each: true`, with `delay`; a `Watch` that rebuilds is no unwatch. Unknown names, private or static methods, parameters and empty lists fail the build.
- `SignalExecutionException` (kind, bean, member, cause; never a value): re-run effects, effects after a flush or a commit and watch callbacks go to the `TypedErrorHandler<SignalExecutionException>` bean, else `FlutterError.reportError`.
- **Security fix:** a `@Computed` value that crossed method security or read the current user is kept for that user only (`spring.flutter.signals.caller-bound`, default `true`); a `Watch` or `@Effect` that read it runs again when the program's user changes.
- `.state`, `refresh()`, `reload()` and `AsyncView` work on the futures of `@Transactional`, `@PostFilter` and `@PostAuthorize` members; a reload runs through the advice.
- A reader of a `@Cacheable` `@Computed` goes on following what it last read after a cache hit. Closing the context no longer reports `SignalBeans` used after being disposed.
- The debug tab and the log show no value of a `@Signal` field or `@Computed` value whose name looks secret (`SecretNames`), as `hidden: true` does.

## 0.8.1

- The first release. `@Signal()` on a field of a component (its readers are told of an assignment; a `List`, `Set`, `Map` or `Queue` also of a change in place), `@Computed()` on a getter or a method without parameters (a value kept until what it read changes), `@Effect()` on a method (run again when what it read changes). The fields stay plain fields.
- Asynchronous state: a `@Computed` getter that returns a `Future` or a `Stream` is loaded once and again when what it read before its first `await` changes; `books.state` (`AsyncValue`), `books.refresh()`, `books.reload()`; an answer that came after a newer request is dropped.
- A method of such a bean is one change, whatever it assigns. `untracked` and `batch` are exported.
- Beside other advice on the same bean (`@Transactional`, `@Cacheable`, an aspect) the advice that follows state has an order of its own (`signalsAdvisorOrder`, one step before the default): on a `@Computed` member the other advice runs when the value is computed, not for each read of the kept one. A rollback does not undo the fields.
- An error is the bean's own: a `@Computed` getter that throws fails its reader with what was thrown (not the wrapper of the signals package), and the failure is not kept (the next read computes again). An effect that throws after its first run is reported (`FlutterError.reportError`) instead of failing the assignment that ran it, and goes on following what it followed; one that throws as the bean is made stops the bean from being made. Other advice on such a bean (`@Transactional`, `@PreAuthorize`) skips the bean's own calls, as in any bean. A `late` field read before it is assigned tells its reader of the first assignment. A collection is followed in place by the field's declared type (`List`, `Set`, `Map`, `Queue`); a field declared as a collection class of its own keeps that class.
- Widgets: `SpringStatelessWidget` and `SpringState` are built again for what their `build` read, by field; `Watch` does the same for ordinary widgets; `AsyncView` shows an asynchronous value.
- The state ends with the bean (its scope is its lifetime); the bean's own `@PreDestroy` methods still read its fields.
- The State tab of the debug panel (`spring.flutter.signals.debug.enabled`): every bean and member by name, with values and how often each changed, loaded or ran; `@Signal(hidden: true)` keeps a value out of it and of the log (`spring.flutter.signals`).
