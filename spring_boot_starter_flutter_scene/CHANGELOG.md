## 0.10.0

- A scene on the engine's canvas leaves it as soon as its route starts to leave (popped, or covered by the next page: its secondary transition runs; a dialog does not run it), not when the transition ends: the engine's canvas cannot fade with the page, so the leaving page's scene stood over the next page for the whole transition (Dota Passport's profile frame over the match page, about 300 ms with a 220 ms fade).
- A scene drawn on the layer's or the engine's canvas builds its widgets under an `Offstage` keyed `PaintedElsewhere`: the debug graph's "On screen" counts them by the scene's place (a `Watch` in a scene is no longer off the screen). A const key: nothing at run time.
- The debug panel's Scenes tab is listed under the menu's `App` group (`GroupedDebugPanelSection`).
- What is not shown is not built: the `scene-fps` segment is in the build only with the console and the scenes (`spring.flutter.scene.enabled`), its parent switches, and when `spring.flutter.debug.console.segments` names it, holds `*` or is not set (its default names it). It is named by `@ConsoleSegment('scene-fps')` in its bean definition; `SceneFpsSegment` has no `name` getter of its own. The scenes' debug tab has the scenes' and the debug panel's switches as parents: a build without the panel has none of its code.
- The debug console's `scene-fps` segment (`SceneFpsSegment`, the `sceneFpsSegment` bean, `springBootStarterFlutterSceneDebugConsoleAutoConfiguration`): the frames a second the 3D scenes draw, shown only while one moves; in the build only with the console (`spring.flutter.debug.console.enabled: true`). `SceneFrames.source` names what the scenes report their frames by (`3D`).

## 0.8.3

- Scenes in Safari and on every browser of an iPhone draw as in Chrome: a half-transparent glow came out there as one block of solid colour, or with a halo. `flutter_scene` hands each frame to Flutter by `transferToImageBitmap`, which Flutter takes as premultiplied; WebKit divides a frame of WebGL by its alpha. Before the engine starts, the starter passes one pixel that way and, where it comes back divided, has CanvasKit take the engine's frames as unpremultiplied (or, without CanvasKit, asks for the engine's canvas without premultiplied alpha); a browser that passes a frame neither way fails the start, so its scenes are their placeholders and not empty. The debug tab says which (**Frames**).
- A moving scene no longer has Flutter draw the page on every refresh of the display: `SpringSceneView` does not tick, every moving view takes its frames from one beat (`SceneFrames`, a bean), and all of them draw on it. A scene of 30 frames a second had the page drawn 60 or 120 times a second, which on a phone is heat and then lag everywhere. In a browser the beat is its frames (`requestAnimationFrame`), elsewhere a timer at the highest rate: a timer beat out of step with the screen, and Safari fired one of 16 ms some 50 times a second (an iPhone scrolling a page with two scenes: 43 → 52 frames a second, the scenes at rest 49 → 60).
- `spring.flutter.scene.canvas: page | layer | engine`: where a frame of a scene goes in a browser. `layer`, a canvas of the scene's own under the page, its frames handed over (`SpringSceneView.canvas` per view); the engine's shared canvas is no longer made anew for each scene of another size where the hand-over is free (Chrome: a beat of two scenes 6.6 → 1.4 ms). `engine`, the engine's own canvas shown as it is: every scene drawn into a texture of its own and the canvas drawn from them where the scenes stand, clipped as the page clips them; behind the page when the first scene fills the screen (one canvas for Flutter), in that scene as a platform view otherwise. Nothing handed over or copied: a beat of two scenes on a Mac 12 → 1.2 ms in Firefox, 6.6 → 1.2 in Safari; an iPhone scrolling, 52 → 60 frames a second.
- `max-frame-rate: 0`: as many frames as the screen shows (60, 120 or 144 by the monitor in a browser, the display's refresh rate elsewhere); `maxFrameRate: 0` on a widget asks for no fewer than the properties let it.
- `spring.flutter.scene.light-clustering`: `false` turns off the engine's clustering of lamps (`Scene.punctualLightClustering`), which made its lamp textures anew whenever a lamp came into the view or left it (a frame of about 30 ms on an iPhone).
- In a web release build the engine no longer marks each render pass on the browser's timeline (`RenderStats.timelineEvents`): the page kept every mark, thousands in minutes.
- The frame meter shows the scenes' frames on a line of their own (`3D 60 fps · 0.9 ms`): drawn without Flutter, they were no frames of its, and the meter said `idle` while they moved.
- `spring.flutter.scene.when`: a condition and the mode it gives every scene while it holds, `still` or `off`; the least of `render` and every condition that holds wins. Two conditions are the starter's, `touch` (a touch screen) and `slow`; an app adds its own as a `SceneCondition` bean. `slow` is decided by the scenes' own frames before they show: while the first scenes load they draw frames nobody sees behind their placeholders, the first few left out (the engine builds its programs on them), the next `slow.probe` measured (what a beat of every moving scene cost the page's thread, not the page's own work); a middle beat over half of `slow.frame-time` and the scenes show in the mode of `when.slow` from their first frame. After they show a scene is never switched off in front of the user: beats slow for `slow.after` mark it, and it holds at the next moment no scene moves (the beats are measured where the scenes draw apart from Flutter's frames, `canvas: engine` or `layer` in a browser; with `page` and on the native platforms only the frames before they show decide). A condition still finding out (`SceneCondition.settled`) keeps the moving scenes behind their placeholders (`SceneAvailability.deciding`). The debug tab shows each, with what it sees. `SpringSceneView.stillOf(context)`: whether the scene a widget is in stands, for a look that has a resting state (a light that strikes when it starts).
- The precision of a material: the build hook finds whether each `.fmat` computes in float32 (`precision highp float;`) or at `flutter_scene`'s default, `mediump`, which is half precision on the GPUs of phones and float32 on a desktop, and the debug tab says it under **Precision** (a warning for `mediump` with no `highp` at all). A material in pixels, seconds or `sin` hashes that looks right on a desktop and wrong on a phone is seen before a phone shows it. The README says when a material wants `highp` (`SceneMaterialPrecision`, `SceneMaterialPrecisions`).

## 0.8.2

- **To do in an app with a scene hook:** what the hook makes goes into `lib/spring_gen/flutter_scene_generated/`, with the rest of the generated code, and no longer into `flutter_scene_generated/` at the app's root. List `lib/spring_gen/flutter_scene_generated/` (and `lib/spring_gen/flutter_scene_generated/opengl_es/` for the web) under `flutter: assets:` in place of the old entries, and remove the old folder. The engine's hooks are run for a stand-in of the app under `.dart_tool/spring_scene/` (`SceneStage`) and their output is copied over; the build says what to add when the entry is missing.
- `buildSceneAssets` (`build_hooks.dart`): one call for an app's `hook/build.dart` that makes the engine's shader bundle with only what the app's scenes draw with, the app's materials (`.fmat`) and its models and scenes (`.glb`, `.fscene`). `buildSceneShaders` is the bundle alone, as before.
- `SceneBackdrop(pixelRatio:)`: the pixels a backdrop draws for one of the screen's, in place of the properties'; a scene of soft light under a whole page costs a quarter at half the pixels. `environment` and `environmentIntensity`: the light around a backdrop's lit materials (0 leaves them to the scene's own lights).
- Materials in the properties, as models are: `spring.flutter.scene.materials.<name>` (`source`: the `.fmat` file the build hook compiles, `preload`). `SpringSceneMesh(material: 'glass', geometry: ..., parameters: ..., everyFrame: ...)` draws with one, a material of its own for every mesh; `SceneMaterials` (`materials['glass'].create()`) gives one to code; the `sceneMaterialLoader` bean makes them. An eager material is loaded with the splash. The build fails when a material of the properties is not among the compiled `.fmat` files, naming the key and the file.
- The debug tab has the materials: file, shading, parameters, how long the first load took and whether its scene waited for it, the uses by scene, a switch that hides one; every scene says which materials it draws with.
- `SpringSceneView.nameOf` and `loadsOf`: the name of the view above a scene widget and the loads it waits for before its first frame, for scene widgets of an app's own.
- - The package's topic `3d` is `graphics`: pub.dev takes no topic that starts with a digit (what was published as 0.8.1 has it already).

## 0.8.1

- The first release. `SpringSceneView` (a view that owns its scene, or the application's) and `SceneBackdrop` (a scene behind a widget) over `flutter_scene`'s `SceneView`: plain widgets over their placeholders where scenes do not draw, a ceiling on frames, a still frame, a pause off the screen, a scene shown only whole.
- `spring.flutter.scene.*`: `enabled`, `render` (`animated`, `still`, `off`), `max-frame-rate`, `pixel-ratio`, `pause-off-screen`, `preload`, `models.<name>`, `app.*`, `debug.enabled`.
- One start of the engine for the app (`SceneEngine`), with the splash (`preload: eager`) or when the first scene shows; `SceneAvailability` says whether scenes draw here.
- The models of the properties (`SceneModels`, `SpringSceneModel`): an asset or an address (read in a browser too), loaded once with the splash or when first shown, cloned by every use.
- The application's scene (`ApplicationScene`, `SceneCustomizer`), under every screen or where the app puts a view.
- The start in application events (`SceneEvent`: the engine, each model, a failure) and in words (`SceneStatus`, the texts by message codes); the splash waits for what is eager (`SceneStart`, a `SplashTask`).
- The engine's own messages (`debugPrint`) as records of the application's log, under the logger `flutter_scene` (`spring.flutter.scene.engine-log`).
- The scenes' tab of the debug panel: the engine, every scene and model by name with what it costs.
- `build_hooks.dart`: `buildSceneShaders`, the engine's shader bundle with only what the app's scenes draw with.
- With `spring.flutter.scene.enabled: false` no scene bean exists and the build leaves the module out.
